A new cold pool parameterization, coupled with a traditional mass flux convection parameterization (Grell-Freitas), was evaluated in the upcoming next generation of the Brazilian weather and climate forecast system called Model for Ocean-laNd-Atmosphere predictioN (MONAN). The new system is based on the Model for Prediction Across Scales (MPAS). This work aims to assess the impact of this parameterization on the trajectory, intensity, and rainfall associated with two tropical cyclones from the 2024 hurricane season, Beryl and Helene. For Hurricane Beryl, a set of sensitivity experiments was conducted to assess the influence of initial conditions, cold pool lifetime, vertical displacement of maximum mass flux, and model resolution (15, 30, and 60 km). The results demonstrated that the inclusion of cold pool dynamics significantly improved trajectory accuracy, particularly at later forecast hours, and enhanced rainfall representation, especially regarding the spatial structure of spiral rainbands and extreme precipitation. Wind intensity forecasts also benefited from a more realistic convective structure, despite persistent pressure biases. An optimal configuration combining reduced cold pool lifetime (1 hour), displaced mass flux peak height, and higher resolution yielded balanced performance across all metrics. This configuration was then applied to Hurricane Helene, where it successfully captured the observed extreme rainfall over the southeastern United States, with improved spatial distribution and overall intensity, and trajectory accuracy. These findings suggest that key cold pool parameters have a substantial impact on tropical cyclone simulations within MONAN, particularly for rainfall forecasting.
