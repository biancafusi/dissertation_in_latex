A new cold pool parameterization, coupled with the traditional mass-flux convection parameterization (Grell–Freitas), was evaluated in the upcoming next generation of the Brazilian weather and climate forecast system, the Model for Ocean-laNd-Atmosphere predictioN (MONAN), which its dynamical core is based on the Model for Prediction Across Scales (MPAS). \textcolor{red}{This initiative lead by INPE aims to develop a unified modeling system capable of producing forecasts across temporal and spatial scales, thereby enhancing weather and climate prediction for Brazil and South America.} This study assesses the impact of the cold pool parameterization on the trajectory, intensity, and rainfall associated with two tropical cyclones from the 2024 hurricane season on the North Atlantic basin: Beryl and Helene. \textcolor{red}{Tropical cyclones are intense, organized low-pressure systems associated with high wind speeds, storm surges, and flooding, making their accurate prediction extremely important for society.} For Hurricane Beryl, a set of sensitivity experiments was performed to evaluate the influence of initial conditions, cold pool lifetime, vertical displacement of the maximum mass flux, and horizontal resolution (15, 30, and 60 km). The inclusion of cold pool dynamics improved trajectory forecasts, particularly at later lead times, and enhanced rainfall representation, especially in the spatial organization of spiral rainbands and the representation of extreme precipitation. Wind intensity forecasts also benefited from a more realistic convective structure, despite persistent pressure biases. An optimal configuration, combining a reduced cold pool lifetime (1 hour), displaced mass flux peak height, and higher resolution, yielded the most balanced performance across all metrics. \textcolor{red}{This configuration was especially effective in representing rainfall structures, particularly accumulated precipitation, a key feature of tropical cyclone impacts.} Applied to Hurricane Helene, this setup successfully captured the observed extreme rainfall over the southeastern United States, with improved spatial distribution, overall intensity, and trajectory accuracy. These findings highlight the substantial role of cold pool parameter settings in tropical cyclone simulations within MONAN, particularly for rainfall forecasting.
