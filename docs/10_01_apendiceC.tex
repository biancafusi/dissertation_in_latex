\chapter{APPENDIX C - DISCUSSION REGARDING THE MAXIMUM DOWNDRAFT HEIGHT}	% trocar A por B na próxima apêndice e etc
\label{apendiceC}	% trocar A por B na próxima apêndice e etc


In this Appendix, we discuss how the downdraft height interacts with the cold pool and, consequently, affects the thermodynamic profile, focusing primarily on temperature and humidity tendencies.

Figure \ref{fig:appendix11} shows the vertical profiles for temperature tendencies (TTCU), humidity tendencies (T(qc+qi+qv)CU), buoyancy-excess tendency (Tend\_buoyx), and the corresponding value of this parameter (buoyx). The black, pink, and green curves represent distinct configurations by modifying the value for the coefficient, which represents the proximity of the downdraft to the surface, given 0.5 (black curve) being the farthest one from the surface and 0.4 (green curve) being the nearest one. On the left panel, the profile was constructed considering an area of land mass, whereas the right panel was constructed considering ocean mass.

One could notice that the black curve tends to cool down less on lower levels, and warm up less on higher levels. On medium levels, it is possible to notice a less humid layer. The combination of these two tendencies lowers the values of the buoyancy-excess parameter when compared to its peers. In other words, when one puts the downdraft farther from the surface (black curve), the effect of the cold pools is reduced (smaller buoyancy-excess), given lesser cooling on the lower levels and a smaller wetting on the medium levels.

In contrast, if one puts the downdraft nearer the surface (green curve), there is a greater cooling on the lower levels, greater humidity on the medium levels, and a warmer layer on the higher levels. This result implies a stronger cold pool, which can be observed both by the tendency of the buoyancy-excess and a greater value for this parameter on lower levels.
